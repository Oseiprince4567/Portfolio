01 / Project Overview

A complete photogrammetric processing chain

The project was completed as a hands-on training case in UAV photogrammetry for land surveying. The portfolio evidence focuses on the processing, control, quality review and GIS use of a supplied aerial image dataset.

02 / Methodology

From image alignment to GIS-ready outputs

A photogrammetric workflow combining tie-point-based image alignment, GCP-constrained bundle adjustment and camera optimisation was used to establish a georeferenced image block. The optimised block was then used for dense point-cloud reconstruction and generation of a DEM and orthomosaic for subsequent spatial inspection, elevation querying and area measurement in QGIS.

03 / Photogrammetric Workflow

Nine stages of the workflow

Selected screenshots document the key processing parameters, block adjustment, control integration, reconstruction steps and GIS-based quality checks. Repetitive interface views were omitted so that each visual documents a distinct technical stage of the workflow.

Stage 01 · Alignment Setup

Image matching and reference preselection

High-accuracy alignment was configured with generic and source-reference preselection. Feature extraction and matching limits were set to 40,000 key points and 4,000 tie points per image to establish the initial image correspondences.

Stage 02 · Block Adjustment

368 images aligned into a single photogrammetric block

Feature matching generated 299,594 tie points, from which the relative image geometry and camera orientations were established for subsequent GCP-constrained adjustment.

Stage 03 · Ground Control Points

Importing and distributing GCPs

Seven ground control points were imported and spatially distributed across the the block. These control observations provided the spatial reference required for the subsequent bundle adjustment.

Stage 04 · GCP Refinement

Moving from approximate to image-level control

Each GCP was inspected in multiple overlapping photographs and manually positioned at the centre of the corresponding ground target. The before-and-after comparison demonstrates the difference between an initially predicted marker position and the image-level observation used for photogrammetric adjustment.

Stage 05 · Bundle Adjustment & QA

Optimising camera parameters and checking GCP residuals

Camera parameters were re-estimated after GCP marking. Metashape displayed a total control-point residual of approximately 0.005 m for the adjusted solution.

Stage 06 · Dense Reconstruction

157 million points reconstructed from depth maps

Depth-map reconstruction generated a dense point cloud containing 157,067,773 points. The resulting 3D dataset represents the reconstructed surface geometry of the survey area, including agricultural fields, roads, buildings and vegetation, and provides the geometric basis for subsequent elevation-model generation.

Stage 07 · Elevation Model

DEM generated at 4.92 cm/pixel

The reconstructed point cloud was interpolated into a 27,858 × 28,015 pixel DEM with a raster resolution of approximately 4.92 cm/pixel. Elevation values displayed within the processed area range approximately from 490 m to 537 m.

Stage 08 · Orthomosaic Generation

2.46 cm/pixel orthomosaic from the DEM surface

The final RGB orthomosaic was generated using the elevation model as the reference surface and mosaic blending to combine the individual images into a continuous georeferenced raster. The resulting product contains 27,647 × 27,136 pixels at approximately 2.46 cm/pixel.

Stage 09 ·Validation & Measurement

Inspecting photogrammetric outputs in QGIS

The exported orthomosaic and elevation raster were loaded into QGIS for spatial inspection and measurement. The orthomosaic was compared with contextual reference imagery, elevation values were queried from the raster, and a field parcel delineated from the orthomosaic returned an ellipsoidal area of 2,201.957 m².

05 / Quality Control & Professional Reflection

Accuracy is more than a small residual

The workflow demonstrates GCP-constrained photogrammetric processing, while deliberately distinguishing the internal fit of the adjusted block from independently verified positional accuracy.

What the 0.005 m value represents

The approximately 0.005 m value displayed in Metashape represents the residual associated with the Ground Control Points used in the adjustment. It indicates a close fit between the photogrammetric solution and the control observations, but it does not constitute an independent assessment of absolute positional accuracy. Because the same control points contribute to the adjustment, their residuals should not be interpreted as equivalent to accuracy obtained from independent validation points.

What a production survey would add

For a production UAV survey, a subset of independently surveyed points would normally be withheld from the bundle adjustment and used as checkpoints. Horizontal and vertical errors could then be calculated against these independent observations and reported using suitable statistics such as RMSE. The control documentation would also record the coordinate acquisition method, reference system, control-point geometry and processing configuration to maintain traceability of the final products.
